# Sell compliance first: 100 Nigerian startup security prospects

A beginner cybersecurity freelancer in Nigeria in October 2026 has a long list of realistic first clients. This report names 100 of them, 48 rated tier A for beginners. The opening offer should be compliance-led documentation and passive hygiene work, not penetration testing. The demand comes from regulators more than from breaches. Almost any startup that processes data on more than 200 people in six months is a "major importance" data controller under the NDPA. It must register, run annual audits and report breaches within 72 hours. Lenders, crypto firms and payment companies carry extra FCCPC, SEC and bank-partner requirements on top. The strongest first targets are small, recently funded or newly licensed firms with sensitive data and no security hire: Duplo, Juicyway, Daya, Gigmile, Lendsqr, Sycamore, Risevest, Roqqu, Blockradar, PaidHR, PowerLabs, Curacel, Heyfood, Platos Health and Edves. Pitch each a fixed-price package of roughly ₦100k–₦1m (these are estimates). Typical contents are a DMARC fix, an NDPA starter pack, a cloud or Workspace hardening review or help with a security questionnaire. Two limits apply throughout. No researcher could open company websites, so security.txt files, VDPs, domains, headcounts and founder handles are all unverified. And 24 of the 100 companies need a 2026 status check before anyone contacts them, because 2025–26 brought a wave of shutdowns.

## Five regulators turn data handling into a buying reason

**The NDPA 2023 and its General Application and Implementation Directive (GAID) are the broadest driver.** The GAID took effect on **19 September 2025** and replaced the old NDPR framework ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/privacy-protection/1606106/the-nigeria-data-protection-commission-issues-the-general-application-and-implementation-directive-2025-gaid)). An organisation becomes a "Data Controller/Processor of Major Importance" once it processes data on **more than 200 data subjects in six months**. Registration costs ₦10,000 to ₦250,000 depending on tier ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/data-protection/1434270/key-highlights-of-the-guidance-notice-on-registration-of-data-controllers-and-data-processors-of-major-importance)). Controllers must run a compliance audit within 15 months of starting business and every year after that. The audit-return fee runs from **₦100,000 to ₦1,000,000**, with a **50% late penalty**. The 2025 filing deadline was extended to **30 May 2026** ([Mondaq](https://webiis10.mondaq.com/nigeria/data-protection/1736914/data-protection-compliance-in-nigeria-audit-return-obligations-for-2026); [Mondaq](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/nigeria/privacy-protection/1770448/regulatory-update-ndpc-extends-data-audit-filing-deadline)). Major-importance entities face penalties of the greater of **₦10m or 2% of prior-year gross revenue** ([TheCable](https://www.thecable.ng/?p=1053071)). Enforcement is real: the NDPC fined MultiChoice **₦766.2m** and Fidelity Bank **₦555.8m** ([ICIR](https://www.icirnigeria.org/ndpc-fines-multichoice-nigeria-%e2%82%a6766-million-over-data-privacy-violations/); [ITEdgeNews](https://www.itedgenews.africa/fidelity-bank-fined-n555-8m-by-ndpc-for-data-protection-violations/)). In August 2025 it began sector sweeps, starting with insurance, pensions and banking ([DataGuidance](https://www.dataguidance.com/news/nigeria-ndpc-begins-sector-sector-investigation)). In February 2026 it probed Temu over excessive app permissions, a theme that applies directly to delivery and commerce apps ([AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202602170066.html)). Health data counts as sensitive personal data, and processing children's data requires parental consent. That makes healthtech and edtech natural NDPA clients.

**The Cybercrimes (Amendment) Act 2024 shortened breach reporting to ngCERT to 72 hours**, down from seven days. Missing the deadline carries a ₦2m fine and/or denial of internet services ([Engineering News](https://engineeringnews.co.za/print-version/entities-will-be-required-to-report-cybercrime-within-72-hours-law-firm-2024-07-19)). That gives every prospect a reason to buy an incident-response playbook. Do not pitch the Act's 0.5% cybersecurity levy, though. The government suspended it on 14 May 2024 and the CBN withdrew its circular three days later ([Channels TV](https://www.channelstv.com/2024/05/19/cbn-withdraws-cybersecurity-levy-circular-after-fg-suspension)).

**The CBN Risk-Based Cybersecurity Framework (2024) binds banks and payment service banks directly.** It requires a CISO, VAPT and resilience controls ([CBN](https://cbn.gov.ng/Out/2024/BSD/CBN%20Risk-Based%20Cybersecurity%20Framework%20for%20DMBs%20and%20PSBs_2024.pdf)). Most fintech startups feel it second-hand, through vendor-risk questionnaires from their sponsor bank. That creates demand for questionnaire support and policy evidence. The CBN's April 2026 supervisory pilot for crypto and payment providers names Juicyway ([RegCompass](https://www.regcompass.com/april-2026-regulatory-roundup/)).

**The FCCPC digital lending regulations (2025) brought every loan app into scope.** They took effect on **21 July 2025** with penalties of up to **₦100m or 1% of turnover**, and they explicitly cover data-privacy abuse ([FCCPC](https://fccpc.gov.ng/digital-lending-fccpc-tackles-abuses-issues-landmark-regulations/)). The approved list had reached **505 lenders** by June 2026 ([PM News](https://pmnewsnigeria.com/2026/06/27/fccpc-expands-approved-loan-app-list-raises-total-to-505/)).

**The Investments and Securities Act (ISA) 2025 treats digital assets as securities.** Exchanges, VASPs and custodians must register with the SEC ([Afriwise](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/isa-2025-what-investors-need-to-know-about-nigerias-new-capital-market-law)). Capital thresholds have risen, a compliance deadline of 30 June 2027 has been reported, and stablecoin rules were proposed in August 2026 ([Chambers](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/blockchain-crypto-assets-2026/nigeria/trends-and-developments)). Every crypto firm on the list therefore needs documented security policies it may not yet have.

The threat backdrop backs up the regulatory pitch. Check Point counted about **4,200 weekly attacks per Nigerian organisation in 2025**, about 60% above the global average ([Guardian NG](https://guardian.ng/technology/tech/nigeria-records-4200-weekly-cyber-attacks-as-attackers-exploit-ai-report/)). Flutterwave reported unauthorised transfers of around ₦11bn in April 2024 ([Techpoint](https://techpoint.africa/2024/05/16/flutterwaves-11-billion-breach/)). The iCredit/BestFin loan app left a **300GB database exposing 846,000 clients** open for almost two months ([Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/security/massive-data-spill-exposes-sms-bestfin-nigeria/)). Apart from the large tier C names and the Chowdeck fake-vendor onboarding investigation, none of the 100 prospects has a publicly reported breach. Researchers read that silence as weak disclosure habits, not as evidence of strong security.

## A beginner sells fixed-scope evidence, not open-ended testing

No published Naira rate cards exist for entry-level security services. The prices below are **estimates** built from regulatory fee anchors and general freelance norms ([Mondaq fee anchors](https://webiis10.mondaq.com/nigeria/data-protection/1736914/data-protection-compliance-in-nigeria-audit-return-obligations-for-2026)). Test them on discovery calls. For contrast, global pentests typically cost **$2,000–$50,000** ([FireCompass](https://firecompass.com/penetration-testing-cost-2025/)), far beyond most Nigerian seed budgets. That gap is why a cheap, fixed-scope package sells. Quote in Naira with a USD reference, and avoid day rates.

The DPCO route explains the last row. Only licensed DPCOs can file audit returns for the two higher tiers of major-importance entities. A DPCO licence requires CAC registration, two qualified staff, a .ng website and an appointed DPO ([NDPC](https://www.ndpc.gov.ng/dpco-registration-requirements/)), and the fee is reportedly **₦2m** ([Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/?p=490236)). In year one a beginner should subcontract to a DPCO. Forming one is a year-two goal at the earliest.

## Written authorisation comes before any test, every time

The Cybercrimes Act criminalises unauthorised access to computer systems ([Templars](https://templars-law.com/app/uploads/2024/08/Cybercrimes.pdf)). A cold email saying "I found a vulnerability on your site" can read as evidence of unauthorised access, or even as extortion. That is the wrong way to open a relationship. Four rules follow.

**First, cold outreach may only cite passive, public observations.** A missing DMARC record, a weak TLS grade or absent security headers all qualify. Port scanning, directory brute-forcing and login testing are off limits until there is a contract.

**Second, nothing is tested without written authorisation.** That means a mutual NDA and an engagement letter with written rules of engagement: targets, dates, allowed techniques, exclusions, an emergency contact and data-deletion terms. The authorisation must be signed by someone who owns the assets. Where hosting providers require it, get their sign-off too.

**Third, sign a data processing agreement whenever the work touches personal data.** The consultant becomes an NDPA processor at that point.

**Fourth, never tie disclosure to a sales pitch.** If you stumble on a flaw, report it privately and free of charge, through a VDP or security.txt where one exists. Paystack runs a HackerOne programme that forbids exploitation beyond proof and public disclosure without approval ([Paystack](https://paystack.com/vulnerability-disclosure)). Moniepoint publishes a security-acknowledgements page ([Moniepoint](https://moniepoint.com/security-acknowledgements)). For the large tier C names, those programmes are the only legitimate way to test.

## Each sector has its own trigger for the first message

The table gives the "what". The sector patterns give the "why now", and that is what makes a cold message worth opening.

**Payments and BaaS firms buy evidence for their partners.** Duplo's new tax-authority licences and Wema Bank integration, Juicyway's CBN pilot and Fincra's new Ghana licence all mean someone else is about to send them a security questionnaire. A policy pack plus DMARC fix solves a problem they already have. Infrastructure players such as Anchor, Maplerad and Miden are best approached with "pass your bank or PCI evidence request", not with testing.

**Lenders and crypto firms buy regulatory paperwork.** For Sycamore, CredPal and Aella, offer an "FCCPC + NDPA readiness pack": privacy notice, consent flows that do not scrape contact lists, a DPIA and a breach plan. For Roqqu, Busha, Quidax, Breet and Blockradar, offer an "SEC registration security pack": an information security policy, a key-management/custody policy, an incident-response plan and staff phishing training. All of these are deliverables a beginner can produce, and the 2027 capital and registration deadline makes them urgent.

**Wealth apps buy account-takeover defence.** Bamboo and Cowrywise had app disruptions during the September 2026 Dangote IPO rush ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2026/09/14/bamboo-cowrywise-explain-dangote-ipo-disruptions/)). High-attention events like that bring impersonation scams, so support-team social-engineering training is a timely offer.

**Logistics, mobility and commerce firms buy BEC and vendor-fraud protection.** In May 2026 a Techpoint investigation got a fake restaurant live on Chowdeck, where it traded until payouts reached ₦100,000 ([Techpoint](https://techpoint.africa/insight/investigation-glovo-and-chowdeck-onboarding-process/)). That gives every marketplace a vendor-onboarding lesson. Freight firms with large B2B invoices, such as Haul247, are natural DMARC clients. Gigmile has the clearest deadline of any prospect: the IFC is weighing a **$4m equity investment** ([Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2026/09/304402-ifc-weighs-4m-investment-in-nigerian-vehicle-financing-startup-gigmile/)), and IFC due diligence asks for governance evidence.

**Health and education firms buy NDPA coverage for special data.** The NDPC partnered with the Health Ministry in April 2025 on health-data reforms ([Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2025/04/18/ndpc-partners-with-health-ministry-to-boost-data-protection-in-nigerias-healthcare-sector/)). It has also opened a probe of tertiary institutions' compliance ([Punch](https://punchng.com/ndpc-probes-tertiary-institutions-over-data-protection-compliance/)). A DPIA plus a children's-consent review is a natural first engagement for Edves, Teesas or Platos Health. AltSchool and Utiva both run cybersecurity programmes, so treat them as instructor or mentor partners rather than clients.

**B2B SaaS, energy and AI firms buy questionnaire readiness.** PaidHR, Lendsqr, PowerLabs, Earthbond and Termii sell to banks, lenders, hospitals or critical-infrastructure operators. Their buyers will ask for ISO 27001-style evidence, and none of them showed a public certification in search results. Energy firms with device fleets, such as Arnergy, PowerLabs and Salpha, should get documentation and cloud work only. Refer any IoT or OT testing to a senior partner.

## A 30/60/90-day plan turns the list into paying clients

**Days 1–30: build the asset base and check the list.**

1. Verify each tier A and B company before contact: website live, recent LinkedIn posts or app updates, and the founders' names and handles.
2. Record each company's security.txt and VDP status by hand in a browser. Note that a missing VDP is itself a pitch point.
3. Pull public DNS records for the 100 domains to measure DMARC adoption. This is passive and legal, and the result doubles as a content-marketing piece ("X% of Nigerian startups can be spoofed").
4. Draft the template package: NDA, engagement letter, rules of engagement, DPA and fixed-price one-pagers.
5. Offer **3–5 tier A startups** a free or discounted authorised "email hygiene + NDPA gap check" in exchange for a testimonial or anonymised case study. The micro-grant and pre-seed firms are the most likely to accept: VARLC, Tespire, Breet, Trove, Shipbubble and eDryv.

**Days 31–60: start sending.**

1. Send founder outreach on LinkedIn or X in batches of 10–15 per week. Lead with one passive observation, one regulatory trigger and one fixed price.
2. Use the investor networks the notes identify for warm introductions:
   - YC: Duplo, Cleva, Heyfood, Topship, Curacel
   - Ventures Platform: Raenest, Juicyway, Fez, Termii
   - Techstars: Gigmile, Treepz
3. Approach one or two DPCOs and law firms as a technical subcontractor. They need delivery capacity, and they are the referral channel for audit work.

**Days 61–90: convert and repeat.**

1. Convert the pilots into paid work. Upsell a cloud or Workspace review and a questionnaire package, then propose a vCISO-lite retainer once there are two case studies.
2. Run a free "security and NDPA basics for founders" workshop through a hub such as CcHUB, Ventures Platform, LoftyInc or Google for Startups. Collect sign-ups.
3. Re-check every tier B "Low ⚑ check" company. Drop the dead ones and fold the survivors into the cadence.

Expect objections and prepare answers:

- **"No budget":** the package costs less than a single minimum NDPA penalty (₦10m for a major-importance entity) or a late-filing surcharge.
- **"We're on AWS/Google":** explain shared responsibility. Misconfigured IAM, MFA gaps and email spoofing sit on the customer's side.
- **"Too small to target":** cite the Check Point attack volumes.
- **"After the raise":** investors and partner banks ask for this evidence during due diligence.

## Credibility comes from cheap certificates and visible community work

The NDPC requires DPCOs to show qualified staff, so certificates have direct regulatory value, not just CV value ([NDPC](https://www.ndpc.gov.ng/dpco-registration-requirements/)). A realistic credibility stack:

1. **A foundation certificate:** ISC2 CC or CompTIA Security+.
2. **A hands-on certificate:** eJPT.
3. **A privacy credential** to support NDPA work.
4. **Later:** ISO 27001 Lead Implementer, for questionnaire and readiness clients.

Community visibility matters as much as paper. The CyberSafe Foundation runs the #NoGoFallMaga campaign and the CyberGirls Fellowship, a one-year programme that drew more than 20,000 applications for one cohort ([GFCE](https://thegfce.org/partners/cybersafe-foundation/); [Cyber Magazine](https://cybermagazine.com/cyber-security/cyber-girls-fellowship-launches-nigeria)). It is a natural place to learn, volunteer and get noticed. OWASP chapters and ISACA Lagos are plausible venues too, but their current activity was not verified.

In-scope findings on Paystack's HackerOne programme give public, checkable proof of skill. Documented pro-bono case studies from the first 30 days give startup-specific proof. Together these let a beginner say "here is work I have done" instead of "trust me".

## What the research could not verify

**Researchers could not open any company website from their environment.** Every fetch of a company domain and of techcabal.com failed at the DNS or proxy layer. As a result:

- **No security.txt, VDP or bug-bounty status was checked** for any company. The only confirmed programmes are Paystack's and Moniepoint's pages, which came up in search.
- **Websites and domains are unverified.**
- **Headcounts are almost entirely unverified.** The only sourced figures are Chowdeck (~259), Heyfood (~40), Crop2Cash (27), Prembly (~80), uLesson and the large logistics firms.
- **Founder LinkedIn and X handles were not collected at all.**
- **No contact emails were found, and none were invented.**

Check all of this by hand before outreach. Several other facts rest on search-engine summaries of articles that could not be fetched, and aggregator figures from Tracxn, PitchBook and Crunchbase often conflict. For example, Kora's funding is given as $400K or $20K, and Shipbubble's as $47.9K or $70K.

Twenty-four companies carry a "Low ⚑ check" flag because no 2025–26 activity was found. Treat them as leads, not targets. The Naira prices are estimates, not market data.

Some regulatory details are also unconfirmed:

- the exact current GAID tier criteria;
- the reported ₦2m DPCO licence fee;
- whether the CBN 2024 framework binds non-bank fintech licensees directly;
- the statute section numbers for unauthorised access.

Get a lawyer's view before relying on any of them in a contract or a pitch.

## Conclusion

The opportunity here is unusual. Nigerian regulators have spent 2025–26 creating paperwork obligations faster than startups can staff for them. GAID audits, 72-hour reporting, FCCPC registration, SEC registration under ISA 2025 and bank vendor-risk reviews all land on companies with 10 to 80 staff and no security lead. That makes documentation the beginner's natural advantage. Policies, DPIAs, questionnaire answers and DMARC records are low-risk and fixed-price, and they produce evidence a client can show. A senior pentester's skills matter less here than reliability and legal care.

The list is perishable. Three of the 2026 shutdowns (Chimoney, LazerPay, Brass) happened within months of this research, and a quarter of the prospects lack fresh proof of life. So the most valuable habit is not a better pitch. It is re-checking status, publishing passive and permission-safe findings as content, and turning the first three to five authorised engagements into case studies that open the next twenty doors.
